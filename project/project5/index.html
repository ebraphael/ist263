Overview of Jewelry and Metalsmithing

What is Metalsmithing?

Metalsmithing is a centuries-old craft defined by the use of various metals, such as iron, coper, gold, and silver, in the creation of objects that are functional or decorative. There are many techniques involved in metalsmithing, including soldering, casting, and texturing, throughout the creation of pieces.

The most popular metals for beginners are copper, brass, gold, bronze, sterling silver, and platinum.

Metalsmithing Tools

Metalsmiths may use hundreds of tools, depending on their level of expertise with the craft and specificity of a given design.

Beginner metalsmithing kits often feature:

  • a jeweler's saw and saw blades
  • pliers (round nose, flat nose, chain nose)
  • various files
  • a variety of sandpaper sticks
  • an anvil or steel bench block
  • a bench pin
  • a chasing hammer
  • a riveting hammer
  • dividers

It is important to note that the metalsmithing practice applies to products beyond wearable jewelry, such as utensils, decorations, and sculptural pieces.

What is Jewelry Design?

Jewelry design is a process that allows for the conversion of ideas into actual, tangible pieces of wearable art. Designs are brought to life through a chain of processes, from conceptualization to the use of beads, metals, and clay.

Jewelry design is a combination of art and science. While it is absolutely a creative practice, there are also several core principles to keep in mind.

Jewelry Design Principles

Balance: Jewelry creation should consider physical and visual weight, though does not need to strive for symmetry.

Emphasis: Many pieces often have a focal point that features a point of distinct contract. This may include a center stone, a certain metal or texture, or an alteration of the piece's shape.

Proportion: Every element of a piece should make sense amongst the other elements. If one area or component is disproportionately sized (too large or too small) compared to the entire piece, the entire product will feel "wrong."

Contrast: Visual interest is created by elements that contrast, like complimentary colors or smooth and textured surfaces.

Unity: Fundamentally, every individual element of a jewelry piece should collaborate to generate a sense of completeness.

The History of Metalsmithing

The Beginning

Metalsmithing tools have been dated back to 9,000 BCE or even earlier. Copper was discovered at a similar time, with silver and gold following not long after. Humans began to use these metals to create everything from currency to art and jewelry.

Humanity used bronze to make armor and swords during the Bronze Age, and the Iron Age saw the beginning of using the smelting process for creating weapons and infrastructure. This is also where fabrication starts to take place.

Innovation and Transformation

By the end of the 15th century, Leonardi da Vinci created a plan for a machine that would roll lead intended for stained glass windows. While this machine was not constructed during da Vinci's lifetime, a similar machine was operational by the 17th century was mostly rolling iron.

The Modern Era

Since the Industrial Revolution, metal fabrication has become increasingly common as a practice for creating common products. With the movement into the information age, technology improved processes like fiber laser cutting and press brake forming, allowing fabrication shops to operate more efficiently.
