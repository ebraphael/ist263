Materials and Metals

Metalsmithing requires the use of nonferrous metals like silver, gold, and brass, due to their resistance to rust, malleability, and susceptibility to shaping. Nonferrous metals are those that contain little to no iron.

Common Metals

Silver: Sterling silver is the industry standard for jewelry due to its strength and bright finish. The metal is 92.5% silver and 7.5% copper. Fine silver (99.9% silver) is softer and more ideal for bezels.

Gold: Gold is measured in karats (indicated by "k"). Pure gold is 24k, while 14k and 18k are alloyed with metals like copper or silver. These alloys are often intended to improve durability and create color variations like white or rose gold.

Copper: Copper is a more affordable metal that is highly malleable and notable for its reddish color. It is a popular choice for practice pieces and wireworks, or as accents on more durable works.

Brass: Brass is an alloy of copper and zinc that is recognizeable for its bright, golden color. It has a more rigid structure and is a budget-friendly option for statement pieces.

Core Properties of Metals

Malleability and Ductility: Some metals, like gold and silver, can be hammered into thin sheets or fine wires without risking breakage. This allows for use in more complex designs and stone settings.

Corrosion and Tarnish Resistance: High-end metals resist oxidation, rust, and chemical breakdown often caused by exposure to air or skin oils. This resistance helps to preserve the appearance of metals over time.

Durability and Hardness: This describes the ability of metals to withstand daily wear without scratching or bending easily. Pure metals are frequently alloyed to optimize this strength.

Hypoallergenic Quality: Platinum, titanium, and high-purity gold can be referred to as "safe metals" because they usually do not irritate sensitive skin or trigger allergic reactioins. Nickel-heavy base metals are often avoided by those with sensitivities.

Aesthetic Luster: A high capacity for polishing gives metal a bright, reflective finish or natural patina. This is odten an attractive feature of jewelry for many shoppers.
